## Business question
What drives profitability?

## Business context
Profit is driven by revenue, by the cost of what was sold, and by operating expenses. A change in any one of the three changes profit, even if the other two stay the same.

## Data required
Revenue is supplied. COGS, discounts, returns and operating expenses are not supplied. Procurement rejection and fulfilment gaps are supplied as volume and order counts, not as rupee costs.

## Analytical approach
Break profit into its parts, then test each part. Without the cost parts, only the revenue and volume parts can be described.

## KPI definitions
Net revenue, gross profit and operating profit, as defined in the KPI file.

## Actual result
Revenue Rs 26,734,294.03. All cost parts: PENDING EVIDENCE.

## Direct answer
No profit driver ranking can be stated. Revenue is the only profit part with a supplied number.

## Business interpretation
The supplied numbers do point to two volume leaks: rejected milk of 1,117,247.35 L, and unfulfilled sales quantity of 20,767.59. Those are volume losses. Their rupee cost was not supplied, so do not convert them to a rupee loss here.

## Recommendation
Supply COGS, operating expenses, and a value for rejected milk and for unfulfilled orders. Then rank the drivers by rupee impact.

## Business impact
Measured impact: none was supplied. Expected benefit: a ranked driver list would show where a fix earns the most.

## Evidence
Evidence: README procurement and sales sections. No cost query output was supplied.

## Business question
Which costs have the greatest impact on profit?

## Business context
A manager must fix the largest cost first. A small cost, fixed well, still earns less than a large cost, fixed partly.

## Data required
No cost breakdown was supplied: no procurement value, no production cost, no wastage value, no returns value, and no operating expense total.

## Analytical approach
Sum each cost by category, divide by net revenue, and rank the categories. Do not rank costs from revenue alone.

## KPI definitions
Expense-to-revenue ratio equals (Operating Expenses / Net Revenue) x 100. Procurement value equals sum of procured quantity x procurement price.

## Actual result
No cost ranking was supplied. Result: PENDING EVIDENCE.

## Direct answer

## Business interpretation
The absence of a cost ranking is itself a finding about the evidence, not about the business. It means the next step is to supply cost data, not to guess a ranking.

## Recommendation

## Business impact
